- License plates: white with red left strip (TR). Black text on white background; narrow rectangular format.
- Language: Turkish Latin alphabet with unique characters: Ğ (silent g), İ (dotted capital i), Ö, Ş, Ü, ç. Words: "cadde/cad." (avenue), "sokak/sk." (street), "yol" (road), "köy" (village).
- Utility poles: concrete poles with 3-arm horizontal top (typical), sometimes A-frame wooden; often with many wires and extra boxes attached.
- Road lines: yellow center lines (dashed); white outer lines. Road quality highly variable.
- Bollards: white with orange reflector band (similar to Greece but note red plate strip).
- Landscape: highly varied — coastal Aegean (Mediterranean, olive groves, resembles Greece), central Anatolia plateau (dry steppe, flat, looks Eastern European), Black Sea coast (green/lush), eastern Turkey (arid mountains, resembles Central Asia).
- Architecture: flat-roofed concrete houses in Anatolia; Ottoman-era stone buildings in historic cities; mosques with minarets (definitive).
- CONFUSION: Turkey↔Romania — Romania has holey concrete poles + Cyrillic occasionally; Turkey has Turkish text + red plate strip. Turkey↔Greece — Greek uses Greek alphabet; Turkey uses Latin with special chars (Ğ,İ,Ş). Minaret = Turkey (or other Muslim country, NOT Romania/Greece).
- REGIONAL COORDS: Istanbul (41.0,28.9), Ankara (39.9,32.9), Aegean coast Izmir (38.4,27.1), Antalya (36.9,30.7), eastern Van (38.5,43.4), central Konya (37.9,32.5).
