- License plates: white with blue EU left strip (FR) + blue right strip with department number. Black text. Unique double-strip design.
- Bollards: distinctive white rounded-top with a FULL reflector band wrapping 360°. Often called "French bollard" — unique in Europe.
- Road lines: thick dashed white outer lines (wider than Spain/Italy); white dashed center on main roads.
- Utility poles: wooden poles with small blue rectangular stickers near the top. Also brown or grey concrete poles with "EDF" markings.
- Road signs: small yellow rectangular signs with D-number (departmental road, e.g. "D45"). Direction signs white with black offset border thicker at the arrow tip.
- Language: French. "Rue" (street), "Route" (road), "Forêt" (forest), cedilla (ç), accents (é, à, è, ê).
- Landscape: diverse — northwest Brittany (rocky Atlantic coast), central plateau (Massif Central, volcanic hills), southeast Provence (dry Mediterranean, lavender), Alps (mountains), Loire valley (flat, wide river), Paris basin (flat agricultural).
- Architecture: limestone farmhouses in center; Provençal stone in south; half-timber (colombage) in Alsace/Normandy; slate roofs in north.
- CONFUSION with Spain: French roads have thicker dashed edge lines. French bollards have full 360° reflector band. French D-number signs are yellow. Spanish roads are cleaner with thinner edge lines.
- REGIONAL COORDS: Paris/Île-de-France (48.9,2.3), Normandy (49.2,0.4), Brittany (48.1,-2.9), Loire (47.4,0.7), Aquitaine/Bordeaux (44.8,-0.6), Provence (43.8,5.4), Rhône-Alpes (45.5,5.0), Alsace (48.5,7.5), Corsica (42.0,9.0).
